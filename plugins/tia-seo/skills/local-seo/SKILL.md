# Local SEO

Local rankings rest on three things Google names itself: relevance, distance and prominence. Distance cannot be changed, so work on the other two.

## Inputs

Business name, website, main service, city or service area, and the GBP link if there is one. Ask for anything missing.

## Step 1: Google Business Profile audit

| Item | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| Primary category | The most specific category that matches the main service (check what top map-pack competitors use) |
| Secondary categories | Only real services; no stuffing |
| Business name | The real-world name only. Keywords added to the name break Google's guidelines and risk suspension |
| Address / service area | Matches the website exactly; service-area businesses hide the address |
| Hours | Complete, with holiday hours |
| Services and products | Every service listed with a short description and price where possible |
| Description | 750 characters max, services and area in plain words, no links or promos |
| Photos | Real photos of the team, work and premises, added regularly |
| Posts | Updates or offers at least every 2 weeks |
| Q&A | Common questions answered by the owner |
| Website link | Points to the matching location page, with UTM tags to track it in analytics |

## Step 2: NAP and citations

- Name, address and phone must match everywhere: website footer, GBP, Facebook, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yelp and the main directories for the country and industry.
- List the top 10-20 citation sources to claim or fix, first the ones that already rank for "[service] [city]".

## Step 3: Reviews

- A steady flow beats a burst. Suggest a simple ask: a short link or QR code after each job.
- Never suggest buying reviews, gating them, or offering rewards for them.
- Write reply templates for 5-star, mixed and negative reviews. Replies mention the service and city naturally and never share private customer details.

## Step 4: Website

- One page per main service, and one page per city only when the business really serves it with unique content (local projects, staff, reviews, photos).
- Title format: `Service in City | Brand`. NAP in the footer, an embedded map on the contact page.
- LocalBusiness schema with address, phone, hours, geo and `sameAs` links to the GBP and social profiles.

## Output

1. A GBP fix list, most important first, with ready-to-paste text (description, services, posts).
2. A citation table: site, status (missing / wrong / OK), fix.
3. Review request message and 3 reply templates.
4. A local page plan: page title, URL, what makes each page unique.
5. LocalBusiness JSON-LD.
