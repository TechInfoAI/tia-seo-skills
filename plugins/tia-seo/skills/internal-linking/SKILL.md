# Internal Linking

Internal links tell search engines which pages matter and how topics connect. The goal: every important page gets links from relevant pages, with anchor text that says what it is about.

## Inputs

- The sitemap URL, a crawl export (Screaming Frog, Sitebulb or similar: source, target, anchor), or a list of URLs with titles.
- The money pages: the 5 to 20 pages that bring sales, leads or the most important rankings. Ask if not given.

## Step 1: Map the site

Group URLs into topics by their title and slug. For each topic pick the pillar page (the broad guide or main service page) and its supporting pages.

## Step 2: Find the gaps

From a crawl export count incoming internal links per URL, then flag:
- **Orphans**: pages with no internal links.
- **Weak money pages**: important pages with fewer links than average.
- **Broken or redirected internal links**.
- **Generic anchors**: "click here", "read more", "this post".
Without a crawl, say that link counts are not known and plan links by topic only.

## Step 3: Plan the links

Rules:
- Supporting pages link up to their pillar; the pillar links down to each supporting page.
- Link where it helps the reader, inside the body text, not only in footers or widgets.
- Anchor text describes the target page; vary it naturally and do not use the exact same keyword anchor everywhere.
- 3 to 5 new contextual links per page is plenty.

## Output

A table the user can work through, most valuable first:

| Source page | Sentence or section to add the link in | Anchor text | Target page | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Then list orphans and broken links to fix, and a short topic-cluster map (pillar -> supporting pages).
